A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Expect gray water (Category 2) or black water (Category 3) in almost any flood, and with it bacteria, contamination and potentially hazardous substances that threaten your health. For flood emergencies anywhere in Gilroy, Gilroy Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. From start to finish we document each step for your insurance claim, with the moisture maps, equipment logs, photos and scope-of-work reports adjusters ask for.

When to Call for Flood Cleanup in Gilroy

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • A water line on walls or baseboards showing how high it rose
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Ground so saturated that puddles sit against the foundation walls

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation
  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.
